## 1. Question and hypothesis

Scout keeps *areas* across refreshes with `areas.Reconcile` (Jaccard of owned-file sets ≥ `match_jaccard`). X2b found no threshold for the production mapper; the design fell back to mapping only at onboarding and manually (rev 2.6/2.7). The user (R-048) asked whether (i) a cheaper, faster mapper — Claude Code `claude-haiku-5-5`, codex `gpt-6.1-terra` at its lowest suitable reasoning effort — and (ii) a Jev (TypeSafe) extension that matches areas by meaning rather than by file overlap would let refresh-on-merge come back.
- **HD5c**: there is a *cell* — a (mapper arm, matching method) pair — for which a match threshold exists at which prior areas are matched by a next-map agent feature for ≥ 80 % of prior areas with ≤ 5 % spurious merges in both fastapi and axum (X2 §1 construct, X2 §4 criteria C1–C3 and gates), that this threshold is confirmed across the two repositories (§7), and whose mapper is not materially narrower than the reference (§7), at a refresh time and Jev cost within the caps (§7).
- Questions for the design: (Q1) which (mapper, method) cell, if any, is adopted (§7); (Q2) for each arm, did the Jev method change the verdict relative to the Jaccard method?

Out of scope: mapper quality beyond the coverage guard (the guard catches a mapper owning fewer files; one owning as many files in fewer, larger areas is not caught and is reported by its area ratio, §7), issue anchoring, inertia keys, other models/efforts/providers, other Clawpatch versions or languages, map token or dollar cost (not measurable, §3), any change to `docs/scout-design.md`.

## 5. Replay, candidate pairs and labels

`run.sh replay` runs X2's replay on all four arms (48 transition rows). `run.sh pairs` (`build_candidates.py`) builds **the candidate set**: for every measured transition of every arm, **every pair (prior area, next agent feature) that shares at least one owned file** (prior areas and next features exactly as the replay defines them: agent features with ≥ 1 owned file). This set contains every Jaccard match at any grid threshold (a match at Jaccard ≥ 0.30 shares files); the script verifies that on the recorded replay (every T_MIN match is a candidate with the replay's Jaccard) and aborts otherwise. It contains in addition the pairs the Jaccard rule never makes — that is where a meaning-based matcher can differ. It writes X2's three files (`blinded.jsonl`, `key.jsonl`, `control-key.jsonl`; same blinded state, side-order hash, pair-id construction, control construction: ⌈8 %⌉ content-swapped and ⌈4 %⌉ identical-record controls of the unique real pairs) plus `jev-input.jsonl` (real pairs only; controls are never sent to Jev). `key.jsonl` carries the file-set Jaccard and the shared-file count of each occurrence. **Every candidate is labelled** with X2's frozen labeller and guide `x2-concern-v1/guide-1`, unchanged (`label.py`; no guide extension was needed: R1–R6 cover the four failure modes read in §0(d)). Labels are `agent_reviewed` only. For Jev cells labels enter (i) the spurious rate per τ (decisive; C2/C3) and, descriptively as X2's frozen analysis computes them for every arm, (ii) the point-biserial r between s and `label == different_concern` over the matches at τ = 0.30 in decisive transitions, (iii) the label distribution by class and by s-band [τ, τ+0.05), (iv) the `different` share in the s-band [0.90, 1.0]. This supersedes X2 §6 "not compared against any Jev output" for X2c only; no other Jev–label statistic (precision/recall curve, AUC, calibration, `tune_threshold.py`, per-label score distributions) is computed or inspected; (ii)–(iv) are exploratory and enter no decision; in Jev tables the "Jaccard" columns are the match score. For Jaccard cells labels are used for the spurious rate (X2 §3.3) and X2's frozen exploratory statistics. The Jev method never sees a label and the labellers never see a Jev score. No human checks them (user decision 2026-10-23, R-039); the labellers are the same families as the mappers (X2 §10 caveat), and the labellers' evidence is the very text Jev is given (§6).
`run.sh replay` and `run.sh pairs` refuse once labels exist (labels are bound to the pair ids).

## 6. The Jev matching method

**Behaviour the user wants and baseline.** Action: decide whether a next-map agent feature continues a prior area (positive = keep the area's identity, so its issues and annotations stay anchored). Baseline: the Jaccard rule (deterministic, free, no egress, blind to meaning). False merge costs more than a miss (X2 §4 rationale: a spurious merge persistently mislabels; a miss mints a new area), so the operational minimums are matched-area survival (C1, ≥ 80 % of prior areas matched) and the different-concern merge rate (C2, ≤ 5 % spurious); they do not establish same-concern precision or recall. Recorded here as **assumed** (the user was not available to confirm; Scout's adapter default is 0.8/0.8, raised on the costlier side): the automated action stays *advisory* (nothing here changes Scout) until the user confirms.

**Pattern.** Entity alignment (TypeSafe cookbook `entity_alignment`): a cheap candidate generator (shared owned file, §5) followed by one judgment per candidate pair; the pair is the state, the answer routes to *different / related / same*.
**Question (one, frozen).** `x2c.concern`, a **Choice** over the four outcomes of the X2 label guide — `same_concern`, `overlapping_concern`, `different_concern`, `cannot_tell` — with each option's criteria **word for word the guide's definition** and the instructions the guide's question and rules R1–R7 (validation compares both with `GUIDE.md`). *Design choices recorded (jev-actions):*
1. **Why not the cookbook's Score.** The Score of the cookbook fits (three ordered outcomes). Scout's client, `internal/decisions` (required by this task: reuse, no second client), sends a Score question *without* its levels (`BuildRequest` empties the criteria of a Score) and the alpha route's handling of Score levels is undocumented; changing the product client is out of scope. A Choice keeps the three outcomes, keeps the guide's own wording as the criteria, yields the whole distribution, and the client supports it with `Positive` and a permuted option order seeded by the unit id (the jaggedness "option order" weak spot). The Jev 1.14 jaggedness page does not exist (404, 2026-10-28); the 1.13 page was not applied to 1.14 **[ASSUMPTION]**.
2. **Why not a Noul per field.** The cookbook's companion Nouls explain which field disagrees for a curator; X2c has no curator and the Noul answers would enter no decision, so none is asked.
3. **State** = what the labellers see: per side `title`, `summary`, `kind`, ≤ 8 `entrypoints`, ≤ 40 `owned_files` and `owned_files_total`, and for the pair `shared_files_total` and ≤ 20 `shared_files`, under the keys `feature_a`, `feature_b`. Secrets are masked with the product's `decisions.Redact`. The instructions carry the data-not-instructions clause and reference state fields by backticked path; the state shape is listed in the question's version key.
4. **Both presentation orders.** The pair is judged twice with `feature_a` and `feature_b` swapped and the score is the mean (jev-actions: "judge both presentation orders and average"); this removes an order artefact (validation S2) at twice the requests, which is a few cents.
5. **Score (main-agent decision 2026-10-28 = council recommendation, round 1 issue 3).** `s = mean over the two orders of [P(same_concern) + P(overlapping_concern)]`, the probability of an answer that X2's C2 does not count as spurious (C2 counts only `different_concern`; rule R6, in the question's instructions, sends ambiguous continuations to `overlapping_concern`; X2b's fastapi matches at 0.30 were 32 of 68 `overlapping`). Thresholds apply to `s`; `jaccard` in the Jev rows carries `s`; the greedy order is (−s, −P(same_concern), prior id, next id), so that among candidates with exactly equal s (e.g. both 1.0) the one with the larger P(same_concern) is matched first; candidates whose s differs at all are ordered by s alone. A Choice reply must give a probability for every option and the probabilities must sum to 1 within a tolerance of 1e-6 (the Choice contract; the product client checks only the supplied values): otherwise the driver records a failure row of kind `schema` and the unit is unscored. A pair is *unscored* when either order has no answer row in `scores.jsonl` at analysis time (the output is append-only and a resumed `run.sh jev` retries failed units; the last answer per (pair, order) is its score, so a failure row counts only while that order stays unanswered): it is never matched and never counted as a pass.
6. **No Jev reasoning, no derivation.** The question asks for a snap judgment from supplied text (no arithmetic, no uncited facts).
**Pin and client.** Model `typesafe/jev-1.14-20261014` (Scout's pin, `internal/policy.DefaultModel`; `x2c.check_jev_pin` refuses a drifted constant), through OpenRouter's alpha/decisions route with Scout's `internal/decisions.Client` (resolved-model pin check per reply: a reply from another model is a `model-mismatch` failure; retries on 429/529; billed `usage.cost` and latency per request). `jev/main.go` is a thin driver (the question, the two orders, append-only resumable output, a billed-cost stop at 5 USD that is cumulative over the append-only output (answer and failure rows with a known cost; a re-run refuses to start once the file's spend exceeds the cap), key from the environment only). The question's version key is `x2c.concern@2ff0e36e…bf1b2` (`x2c.JEV_QUESTION_VERSION`; validation compares it with `jev -print-version`). The key (`OPENROUTER_API_KEY`) exists only through `infisical run --projectId 052b4c81-b9cc-4694-9019-d131ec3817cc --env dev --silent -- <command>`; it is never printed, exported or written (validation: it appears in no output, log or request body). The driver is built by `go run` from the working tree, so `run.sh jev` appends the SHA-256 of every non-test file of `internal/decisions`, of `internal/policy/consent.go`, `internal/areas/areas.go`, `internal/clawpatch/records.go`, `go.mod` and `go.sum` to `data/jev/code-digests.jsonl` before each driver run, and the analysis reports those lines; RESULTS states their equality with the sign-off blobs.
**Matching.** For a mapper arm A and threshold τ on the 0.30–0.90 grid, the Jev method matches prior areas to next features **greedily best-first and one-to-one** over the candidates with `s ≥ τ`, ordered by (−s, −P(same_concern), prior id, next id): the same greedy best-first one-to-one rule as `areas.Reconcile`, with `s` in place of the Jaccard and ties broken by (prior feature id, next feature id) rather than Reconcile's (area id, feature id); a product implementation would use area ids, which differs only on exact ties (`jev_match.py`). The resulting rows have the Jaccard rows' schema with `arm = "<A>+jev"`, `jaccard` carrying `s`, `created = next features − matched`, `kept = prior areas − matched`, `retired = 0` (schema placeholders, unmeasured: they enter no decision and every report says so, §12); X2's analysis then decides the cell with its own rule. Greedy best-first one-to-one makes the matches at τ exactly those at 0.30 with `s ≥ τ`, so labels are threshold-independent as in X2.
**Dev/test.** jev-actions asks for thresholds tuned on a development unit and scored once on another, split by an independent unit (repository). X2's `t* = max(pass)` is selected on the same data it is judged on; §7 adds the cross-repository confirmation for *both* methods. No threshold is fitted with `tune_threshold.py`; the grid and rule are X2's. The scorer writes `data/jev/questions/<id>@<version>.json` (the question as sent) and `data/jev/scores.jsonl` (raw per-request scores, the `scores.csv` analogue); the assumed minimums above stand in for `thresholds.json` (no threshold is tuned by the skill's script, so no other file of the protocol is produced).
**Jev gates (reported; they decide eligibility of Jev cells, §7).** Model = pin and question version = `x2c.JEV_QUESTION_VERSION` on the header and every answer row; every answer row reports a billed cost; these fail every Jev cell. A `model-mismatch` failure row (the client rejected a reply from another model, so no answer row can carry one) and a failure row without a billed cost are retained and reported (`failures_by_kind` and `cost_unknown_rows` in `jev_requests`, `foreign_model_failure_rows` in `jev_check`), not gated; their unit is unscored, and counts in that arm's unscored share, only while that order has no answer row (a resumed run may answer it); unscored share ≤ 1 % of candidate occurrences per (arm, repository), a breach failing the Jev check of that arm only (the header/answer-row pin, question-version and billed-cost checks fail every Jev cell); per-refresh Jev cost (both repositories summed, mean over transitions) ≤ 0.10 USD and per-refresh Jev time ≤ 300 s, where Jev time = Σ request latencies ÷ 8 **[ASSUMPTION: a product Jev matcher issues 8 concurrent requests; `decisions.Run` is sequential today, run.go:186; the sequential sum, `seconds_sequential`, and the share of answers with `attempts` > 1 are reported beside it]**. The per-refresh cost and time are answers-only means over the measured transitions (a measured transition without candidates counts as 0); failure rows enter only the reported total billed cost. **[ASSUMPTION: Scout's `max_cost_usd` default is 0.50 per run; a refresh must leave room]**.

## 7. Decision rule (preregistered; per cell, then selection)

A **cell** is a (mapper arm, method) pair: 8 cells.
**Per-cell verdict** — X2 §4 unchanged: C1 per-repository mean survival ≥ 4/5 (exact), C2 per-repository spurious rate ≤ 1/20, C3 Wilson upper bound ≤ 0.10; class gate, label gate (≤ 1/10 unlabelled), control gate (pooled over every arm and method: one labelling session, one set of controls), then `adopt t* = max(pass)`, else `inconclusive` (imprecise), else `fallback`. A non-contiguous pass set, the survival margin and the unlabelled share at t\* are reported.
**Citable** (replaces X2's probe-consistency flag, as X2b's served/flags check did): the **served check** of the cell's arm passes — every probe (before and after, both repositories) names the arm's model and effort, every versions file and `meta.json` records the arm's flags, every successful map's `agent-start` reported the arm's provider and model option — **and** X2's probe-consistency flag for the arm is consistent, **and** (Jev cells) the Jev check passes (§6; an unscored-share breach counts only for its own arm), **and** the replay contains no arm outside the four. A cell that fails citability is `inconclusive` for the selection, whatever its verdict.
**Eligible** (a citable `adopt` cell must also pass all of):
- *Cross-repository confirmation* (both methods): for each repository R, `t_R` is the largest grid threshold at which the **other** repository alone is `pass` (X2 classification); R is scored at `t_R` on C1 and C2 (exact; C3 is not applied to the held-out half). Confirmed iff both directions have a `t_R` and both pass. A cell without it is `ineligible`.
- *Coverage guard* (mapper, both methods): the arm's mean owned files per map (over measured transitions, (prev + next) ÷ 2) ≥ **0.5** × the reference arm's in each repository. **Decision (main agent, 2026-10-28; the council's option B, round 1 issue 1):** the user asked for haiku and the lowest suitable terra effort to be compared (R-048), and a floor of 0.8 would exclude them by construction; 0.5 keeps the guard's purpose (a mapper cannot win by mapping least). [ASSUMPTION, set after the smoke counts were seen: against X2b's luna maps (owned files at T1: fastapi 243, axum 224; R-042) the single smoke maps give, fastapi/axum, haiku default 109/160 (≈ 0.45/0.71), haiku low 66/157, terra-low 585/169 (axum ≈ 0.75), terra-none 412/216 (≈ 1.7/0.96); at a floor of 0.8 the guard would be expected to make both haiku cells and terra-low's axum cells ineligible unless the real maps differ; at 0.5 only haiku default on fastapi (≈ 0.45, one draw) is expected just below it.] The **area ratio** (mean of (prior areas + next agent features) ÷ 2 per map relative to the reference's, per repository) is reported, not gated (smoke axum agent features: terra-none 41, terra-low 32, haiku 21 against luna's 86 in X2b). A reference arm unmeasured in a repository makes the guard not evaluable (every cell `ineligible` on that gate) and, through X2's class gate on the reference's own cells, the outcome `inconclusive`.
- *Jev caps* (Jev cells): §6.
- *A recorded map time* for the arm.
**Selection** (`analysis_x2c.select`): among eligible cells take the smallest **refresh time** (map time per refresh, §3, plus, for Jev cells, Jev time per refresh); cells within 10 % of that minimum are *tied*; among tied cells prefer the **Jaccard** method (deterministic, free, no egress), then the larger survival margin at t\*, then the arm earlier in the table of §3. Output `adopt <arm> with the <method> method: match_jaccard = t*` (or `Jev match threshold tau = t*`), "for a design round, not adopted here; specific to this arm's served model and effort".
**Outcomes.** `adopt` as above. `no-adoption`: no cell is eligible and none is `inconclusive` (every cell is `fallback`, or `adopt`-but-`ineligible`): the design's fallback stays (agent map at onboarding and manually; no refresh-on-merge). `inconclusive`: no cell is eligible and at least one cell is `inconclusive` (an unmet gate, an imprecise rate, a failed served or Jev check, an unmeasured reference): a new preregistration is needed. A cell that is `inconclusive` while another is eligible does not block the selection; it is reported.
**What counts as inconclusive, restated.** Any X2 gate (class, label, control — the control gate being pooled, a failure makes *all* cells `inconclusive`); an `imprecise` threshold set; a failed served check; a failed Jev check; fewer than the two repositories measured.
**Consequence for the design (a later council round, not this study).** `adopt` with the Jaccard method at t\* proposes `mapping.areas.match_jaccard = t*` and the arm's `mapModel`/`mapReasoningEffort` (a provider change is a design change); `adopt` with the Jev method would add a decisions question to the refresh (a new product feature, `provisional`, with the minimums confirmed by the user); `no-adoption` leaves rev 2.7 as is. A Jev `adopt` requires a concurrent request runner in the product (`decisions.Run` is sequential today, run.go:186), a product change for the design round. An `adopt` whose arm's area ratio is < 0.8 in either repository proposes a coarser area granularity; the design round decides whether that is acceptable before citing the cell. If X2c's luna-xhigh Jaccard verdict differs from X2b's, RESULTS states that one-draw verdicts for this configuration are not reproducible, HD5b's state is unchanged, and the proposal line carries "X2b (same configuration): fallback". X2/X2b remain the evidence for luna/xhigh with the Jaccard method; X2c's luna-xhigh cells are a second, independent draw of that configuration (reported beside X2b's, never pooled with it).
**Ledger state (HD5c).** `adopt` and citable → `supported` (that cell, grid, guide); `no-adoption` → `not-supported` (existential; two repositories, one run per arm); `inconclusive` → `inconclusive`. The ledger outcome states, for the selected cell, mean survival per repository at t\* (exact) and the spurious rate with its Wilson 95 % interval; for `no-adoption`/`inconclusive`, the same at each cell's best threshold; `supported` is scoped "(spurious merges as labelled by `agent_reviewed` labellers, R-039; for Jev cells an internal-consistency result with labellers following the same guide, R-038)". The effect column of the HD5c row is "—". For a non-reference arm the ledger outcome states its area ratio per repository; where it is < 0.8 in either repository, `supported` is further scoped '(areas coarser than the reference's, ratio …; §7 Consequence)'.

## 8. Uncertainty, multiplicity, unmeasured

X2 §7 unchanged for every cell (Wilson intervals, per-refresh mean survival, unlabelled counts). Eight cells and 13 thresholds are examined on the same labels; selection of t\* over nested thresholds is not removed (X2 §7). The cross-repository confirmation removes part of the in-sample selection for the survival/spurious criteria. With one run per arm and repository the run-to-run variance of each mapper is inside its estimate; the luna-xhigh cells repeat X2b's configuration, so their agreement with X2b is the only replication-like information (descriptive). The four arms share repositories and labels: differences between cells are not independent. No family-wise correction across the 8 cells (cells of one arm share its maps; all share the labels); HD5c `supported` is existential. RESULTS reports the number of cells examined, the number eligible, and, per cell, the largest over the 13 grid thresholds and the two repositories of the probability that one repository with that threshold's realised labelled count meets C2/C3 at true spurious rates 0.08 and 0.10 (X2 `operating_characteristics` formula; the same rule for every cell, independent of its verdict), with their sum over cells; single-threshold figures conditional on C1 with independent matches, so the sum approximates and does not bound the family-wise false-support probability. The confirmation tests whether a threshold tuned by X2's rule on one repository alone meets C1/C2 on the other; it does not hold out the `adopt` verdict or t\*, cannot see a labeller bias shared by both repositories, and is not monotone (a wider pass set in one repository raises `t_R` and can fail the other).

## 11. Threats

- **Labels are agent labels** from the mapper families, evidence shared with Jev: a shared blind spot would raise Jev's apparent agreement. X2 §10 applies. The Jev method and the labellers answer the same question from the same text; agreement is therefore an *internal consistency* result, not proof of correctness. It is the intended comparison (no human labels exist), stated here so no design cites it as more.
- **One run per arm and repository; four arms; eight cells on shared labels** (§8).
- **Haiku `none` is "the model default"**, not a fixed effort; if the default changes server-side the probe cannot see it.
- **The coverage guard is a coarse proxy for mapper quality** (owned-file volume); a mapper can pass it and map the wrong things; mapper quality is out of scope. It counts files, not areas: a mapper owning as many files in fewer, larger areas passes it and, if fastest, is selected; the area ratio is reported (§7) and a ratio below 0.8 sends the question to the design round. The 0.5 floor was set after the smoke counts were seen (§7).
- **Jev time is an 8-worker service-time estimate, not an observed refresh wall time**, and the product client is sequential today (`seconds_sequential` is reported); the synthetic planted Jev scores in validation test arithmetic and wiring, not Jev.
- **The Jev score `s` includes `overlapping_concern`** (§6 item 5): it matches the answers X2's C2 accepts, so a Jev `adopt` may match ambiguous continuations that a stricter reader would call different areas; the labels, not `s`, decide the spurious rate.
- **Jev cost/time caps are assumptions**; the smoke gave no Jev figure. Latency depends on OpenRouter load at the time.
- **The candidate set is larger than X2's** (every file-sharing pair): the control gate pools over all of it; the unlabelled and control rules are unchanged.
- **`gpt-6-luna`, `gpt-6.1-terra` and `claude-haiku-5-5` are names that may roll**; probes bracket each collection but cannot see a same-name change.
- **Concurrency of the smoke** inflated its wall times; the real run is sequential.
- **Knowledge of X2/X2b/smoke** (disclosure above).
- **Fixture sources moved** (§2): any clone with the pinned revision is accepted; the revision SHA, not the path, is verified.
